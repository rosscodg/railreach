      Frequently asked questions

      What is the fastest train commute to London?

      Battersea Park reaches Victoria in 4 minutes, Limehouse reaches Fenchurch Street in 4 minutes, Vauxhall reaches Waterloo in 4 minutes, Kentish Town reaches St Pancras in 5 minutes, New Cross reaches London Bridge in 5 minutes. These are the quickest scheduled services in the dataset, measured from published timetables rather than estimated.

      Which London stations can I commute to?

      RailReach covers 11 London terminals: Kings Cross, St Pancras, Waterloo, Paddington, London Bridge, Victoria, Liverpool Street, Euston, Marylebone, Fenchurch Street, Moorgate. Moorgate and St Pancras matter more than their profile suggests: several Great Northern suburbs run to Moorgate rather than Kings Cross in the morning peak, and the Medway towns reach St Pancras on high speed services far faster than they reach any other terminus.

      Which commuter towns are within 30 minutes of London?

      Battersea Park (4 min to Victoria), Limehouse (4 min to Fenchurch Street), Vauxhall (4 min to Waterloo), Kentish Town (5 min to St Pancras), New Cross (5 min to London Bridge), Clapham Junction (6 min to Victoria), Finsbury Park (6 min to Kings Cross), Stratford (6 min to Liverpool Street), Acton Main Line (7 min to Paddington), Lewisham (7 min to London Bridge), Ealing Broadway (8 min to Paddington), West Hampstead Thameslink (8 min to St Pancras). Each figure is the fastest scheduled weekday service.

      Why do you show a typical peak time as well as the fastest?

      Because the fastest train is often not the one you can catch. Higham is 42 minutes at best but typically 81 in the peak, Chestfield & Swalecliffe is 79 minutes at best but typically 110 in the peak, Rainham (Kent) is 49 minutes at best but typically 78 in the peak, Gillingham is 43 minutes at best but typically 72 in the peak. The typical figure is the median journey time of services arriving between 07:00 and 09:30, which is what a commuter actually experiences.

      How accurate is the journey time data?

      Every figure is computed from Darwin timetable files published by the Rail Delivery Group, sampled across three midweek days and matched to stations by TIPLOC. Passing points, empty stock movements and cancelled services are excluded. Times are scheduled, not live, and do not account for delays or engineering work.
